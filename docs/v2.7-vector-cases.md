# v2.7 - vector extraction: findings and draft cases

**Status: DRAFT for David to agree (2026-10-12).** Nothing here is built.
Gate 1: every case below is written as "given X, the screen shows Y". A case
that cannot be written that way is still a conclusion and is marked as an open
question instead.

The full research record (sources, verification marks, the experiment files)
is private: `plottracer-ref/research/v2.7-vector/FINDINGS.md`.

## Why this release

The roadmap entry: PDF and SVG figures, where *"the bigger win is the AXES,
not the paths: in vector form the tick labels are real text with real
positions, so the calibration can be READ rather than clicked or OCR'd."*

A vector figure is the one case with a single transcription instead of two:
the path is the author's own geometry. It is a better instrument for the same
record, not a new kind of record.

## What we found (short)

**Prior art (tenet 11b).**
- **No digitizer we know of reads vectors.** WebPlotDigitizer renders a PDF page to a picture at 3× and works from pixels (read in its code); Engauge, PlotDigitizer and the others rasterise too.
- **Two research tools do read vectors:**
  - **ChartDetective** (CHI 2023), semi-automatic, on our stack (PDF.js, TypeScript, Tesseract). Its licence is not checked yet, so its code stays unread.
  - **figverify** (2026), fully automatic, scatter and line only.
- **Hand methods exist and are scripted:** PDF to SVG, pick a series by colour, take marker centres, calibrate from the tick marks.
- **The useful lessons:**
  - Resolve every shape to its final position once.
  - Split compound paths.
  - Let the user say what a group of shapes is, and measure the geometry.
  - Calibrate from the label text, and refuse ("uncalibrated rather than guessed") with fewer than two good ticks or a bad fit.
  - The printed labels' rounding, not the coordinates, limits precision.
- **Nobody** pairs each label with its own tick mark and cross-checks the two, shows data clipped outside the plot, or tells the user up front whether a figure is drawn as vectors at all.
- ⚑ **Most published figures are pictures inside the PDF:** one study found only 15 of 136 vector. The vector path is the better one when it exists, never the only one.

**The reverse (tenet 11a)**, from figures made and inspected here. Matplotlib, R, ggplot2 and LibreOffice were checked; gnuplot, MATLAB, Origin and Excel are still to check with real files.
- **Matplotlib and R:** one path per series, its corners exactly the data points.
- **LibreOffice:** a *smoothed* curve of about 100 segments between 6 data points, so a path's corners are not always the data.
- **Markers** come four ways (a reused symbol, an inline circle, a 100-sided polygon, a `<use>`), often painted twice.
- **Tick labels are real text in every PDF made here.** In SVG, matplotlib and R write them as letter outlines by default.
- **Every data line is clipped to the plot area.**
- **The legend's sample line is drawn exactly like its series.**
- **A PDF page brings captions and other figures with it.**

**PlotTracer today.**
- A PDF page becomes a picture at about 3.7×. The scale isn't kept, and no path or text is read.
- The PDF itself is kept in the project. An SVG's is not.
- Of the edits after the render, only crops are recorded; rotate, flip and deskew are not. So nothing can map a drawn coordinate onto the working picture yet.
- A vector series has a home already: Find lines' result (one series per line, a role per point).
- Read tick labels have one too: the OCR review card (nothing applied until Apply).

## Principles for this design

1. **Mirror, don't add.** The user calibrates and extracts with the tools they already have:
   - the calibration card;
   - Find lines and Find this line;
   - the bar reader;
   - Places to check;
   - masks.

   When the figure is drawn as vectors, those tools read the drawing instead of the pixels, and say so. No second set of tools.
2. **Assert only what was measured; everything else is OFFERED.** A label's text and its tick mark's position are both measured. Pairing them is offered and confirmed with Apply, as OCR's reads are.
3. **Record what is drawn** (tenet 9). A line's corners are the drawn geometry; a marker's centre is a drawn position. Neither is called "the data" unless the figure shows it.
4. **Refuse where unsure, and say why, at the gesture.**
5. **The picture stays the reference.** Everything read from the drawing lands in the same pixel space as the picture and is drawn over it, so the eye can check it as before.

## Draft cases

### Telling what the figure is

- **V1.** Given a PDF page whose captured figure is drawn as vectors, the
  calibration card says: *"Drawn as vectors: PlotTracer can read its lines
  and labels from the drawing."*
- **V2.** Given a captured figure that is a picture inside the PDF, the card
  says: *"A picture inside the PDF: read from its pixels, as any image."*
  Everything works as today.
- **V3.** Given a capture that holds both, the card says which: *"Partly a
  picture: the lines are drawn, the background is a picture"*, or the reverse.
- **V4.** Given a PNG or JPEG, nothing new shows.

### Calibration read from the labels

- **V5.** Given a vector figure whose tick labels are real text, the calibration card
  offers *"Read the axes from the figure's labels"*. Pressed, it shows each label
  it read beside its own tick mark on the canvas: X 0, 20 … 100 and Y 0, 500 …
  3500. Nothing is applied until Apply, as OCR's reads are reviewed.
- **V6.** Apply fills the calibration with the paired tick marks and the
  labels' values, exactly as if the user had clicked those ticks and typed
  those values. Calibrating by hand still works and is unchanged.
- **V7.** A label is paired only with a tick mark drawn at it. A label with no
  tick mark, or a tick mark with no label, is shown as not used, with the reason.
- **V8.** An axis with fewer than two usable label-and-tick pairs, or whose
  pairs do not fit one linear or one log scale, is not offered: *"Y could not
  be read: …"*. It is never guessed.
- **V9.** Given labels like 10², 10³, 10⁴ (an exponent written smaller and raised),
  the offer reads them as 100, 1000, 10000, and offers the axis as log, with the
  log option ticked and visible.
- **V10.** Given tick labels drawn as letter shapes, not text (the SVG default
  of matplotlib and R), the card says: *"The labels are drawn as shapes, so
  they cannot be read from the drawing"*. It offers the existing OCR read
  instead.
- **V11.** The offer says how precisely the labels pin the scale: *"to the
  printed digits: 0.5 on X, 50 on Y"*. That rounding, not the drawing,
  limits it.

### Lines and points read from the drawing

- **V12.** Given a calibrated vector figure, Find lines on a colour reads the
  drawing's paths of that colour inside the plot area. Each line becomes its
  own series, as today. The card's summary says *"read from the figure's
  drawing"*, and each point carries that as its source in the export.
- **V13.** Find this line on a vector figure takes the path under the click.
- **V14.** Given a line whose path has many more corners than the figure has
  markers along it (LibreOffice's smoothed curve), the result says so: *"This
  line has 600 corners and 6 markers; the markers may be the data"*. It offers
  the markers as their own series. It never decides for the user.
- **V15.** Given markers (a scatter, or markers on a line), picking a marker's
  colour, or clicking one, gives one point per marker at the marker's centre. A
  marker painted twice (filled and outlined) counts once.
- **V16.** Given a path that runs outside the plot area, where the figure clips
  it, the parts outside are not added. The summary says how many points were
  outside, and Places to check shows where.
- **V17.** Shapes inside a masked area (a legend) are left out, as Find lines
  does today on pixels.

### Bars

- **V18.** Given a bar chart drawn as vectors, the bar reader takes each bar's
  rectangle. The height comes from its top edge, at the category its middle
  falls in, and the bar is checked to stand on the common baseline. A bar that
  does not is listed under Places to check. The record is the bar's height (David, 2026-10-12: *"All they need is the
  height, and be reasonably sure they are reaching a common bottom."*).

### Keeping the drawing and the picture together

- **V19.** Given a vector figure that is cropped, rotated, flipped or
  deskewed after capture, everything read from the drawing lands on the same
  place in the picture as before the edit. A re-read after the edit lands
  there too.
- **V20.** Given a saved project from a vector PDF or SVG, reopening it can
  still read the drawing. The SVG is kept in the project, as the PDF already is.

### Agents

- **V21.** get_state says whether the figure is drawn as vectors (V1-V3), and
  an agent's find_lines and calibration offer read the drawing the same way as
  the user's.

### The reverse check (a test, not a screen)

- **V22.** For a matplotlib line and scatter figure, saved as PDF, the series read from the drawing
  equal the data the figure was made from, to the printed precision. A
  calibration from the labels equals the figure's own axis limits, to the
  labels' rounding. The same for R's `pdf()`, and for every generator we get a real file from.

## Open questions for David

1. **Order and scope.**
   - My proposal:
     - **phase 1**: PDF, the "vector or picture" line, and the calibration from the labels (V1-V11);
     - **phase 2**: lines, markers and bars from the drawing (V12-V18);
     - **phase 3**: SVG (V20; it needs our own path reader).
   - The provenance fix V19 comes first, because nothing can be placed without it.
2. **Under the existing tools, or a tool of its own?** I recommend under the existing ones (principle 1): the same buttons read the drawing when there is one, and say so.
3. **A line's corners or its markers** (V14), when both are drawn: offer both and let the user choose, or default to one?
4. **Sharp zoom:** while the drawing is there, should the picture re-render sharp when zoomed in? It is not needed for the data, and could be left for later.
5. **Real files from gnuplot, MATLAB, Origin and Excel.** Do you have figures from papers that use them? A handful of real published vector figures would be the test set.

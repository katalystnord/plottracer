# v2.7 workflow walk (from 2026-10-30)

David walks the workflow step by step in the built app; each step records what did not work and what should
change, in his words where possible. Only his decisions are written here. The rules it is held to (David,
2026-10-21): automatic tools live inside the existing cards and tools and produce the same objects the manual
way does; manual and automatic steps mix seamlessly. And (David, 2026-10-31): **one workflow and one UI layout
for every kind of figure** (drawn PDF, PDF page as a picture, PNG, JPEG, scan); whatever is settled holds for all of
them. (Later, not now: "PDF" becomes PDF + SVG.)

⚑⚑ **There is no separation between an automatic and a manual flow** (David, 2026-10-31: "This is really important
that you see this"). There is one card per step and, in it, ways of doing the step: a click, the guided order,
a detection. Each produces the same objects, any of them can finish or correct what another started, and nothing
records which one was used. Never write "the manual walk" or "the automatic path" as if they were two things.

## The graph type suggestion (David, 2026-10-31)

The classifier runs directly, in the background, as soon as the figure is captured (Capture figure), so it
classifies the figure it was measured on, never a whole page. It only highlights a suggested type
(green, in the graph type picker); the user chooses. "For a normal user it is not so important, but for the full
automatic flow later it will be crucial."

The cases (2026-11-04, built from the above; tests in ui/__tests__/workspace.e2e.test.ts, "the graph type suggestion"):
- Given a bar chart captured (bar-tensile-strength), the graph type picker shows the Bar card outlined green with
  "Suggested" under its name; the graph type in use does not change.
- Given a figure the classifier is not sure of, or one of a type it cannot name (spider-material-profile), no card is
  green.
- Given a suggested type and a new image opened, the suggestion is gone; it comes again only from a new Capture
  figure. A project opened from a file has no suggestion (it was not captured).
- Given the classifier cannot run (no worker, a refusal), no card is green and no warning is shown: the suggestion
  is an offer, and the user choosing the type is the normal path.
- The suggestion is the measured fusion in engine/figureType.ts at 70% (29 bundled samples pinned in
  engine/__tests__/figtypeResize.test.ts); it runs untracked, so the status ring does not turn for it.

## Step 3: the calibration card (the final design, David 2026-10-31)

  - a point that was not found can still be placed: the user clicks its chip in the calibration card (X2, say),

- The calibration card is the Calibrate tool's fold-out, detached from the rail so it is always visible, at least

## The Legend tool's card (rail 3, David 2026-10-31)

Only three buttons, top to bottom: **[Mark legend]**, **[Auto detect legend]**, **[Mask an area]**.

- Nothing that is read from the legend is shown to the user ("It will just confuse people"). What is read is
  used only to offer series names as suggestions, and by the internal tools to help the automatic series
  detection.
- The only thing shown is the legend's corners, whether marked or auto-detected.
- Everything developed toward this comes into the workflow branch (David, 2026-11-01: "we need to bring in
  everything that we have developed that is part of solving this. I do not care where it is stored."): ChartDete
  and the graph-type classifier in the app's runtime, picture axes with labels lead, label vetting, the tick
  finder, the warning triangle. The separate automatic path's cards stay behind (2026-10-21).
- A marked or detected legend is masked by itself: "that is half the point." Mask an area stays for everything
  else that is not data (text, arrows, insets, reference lines). Marking the legend by hand is the same
  corner-to-corner drag as Mask an area and crop.
- A masked area and a marked legend are shaded in different colours, so they can be told apart.
- A mask no longer paints the area out of the picture (David, 2026-11-01: "then we change this of course"). A
  masked area and the legend are rectangles laid over the figure: the picture stays as it was, every reader sees
  the rectangles blanked out, and each keeps its corners, editable while the Legend card is open.
- If Auto detect legend finds nothing, a warning with the triangle in the bottom bar says so, as the calibration
  card's detection does.
- Selecting a legend corner lets the user edit it while the Legend card is open; with the card closed it cannot
  be selected or changed. The same as the calibration card.

### The study, measured

How common it is, on the CHART-Info PMC test corpus (both splits; series inks sampled along each ground-truth
polyline and at its vertices, "same ink" within the harness's own colour tolerance, Chebyshev 30):
- 312 line figures, 271 with two or more series; in 85 of them (both ink measures agree) two or more series share one
  ink, 53 of those with legend marks. Checked by eye on a random 12: 11 really share the ink.
- The benchmark's line score on those 85 is 45.7% found, against 80.8% on figures whose series have distinct inks: the
  largest single gap in the line results.
- What tells them apart, by eye, in the 38 same-ink legends: the dash pattern alone 18, dash pattern and marker 11,
  marker alone 9. Dotted lines appear in 9 figures; dash-dot or custom dashes in about 4.

Prior art (Tenet 11): matplotlib, R and ggplot2 all define a style as an on/off sequence along the line, in units of
its width (matplotlib '--' is (3.7, 1.6), '-.' (6.4, 1.6, 1, 1.6), ':' (1, 1.65); R and ggplot2 hex strings "44",
"13", "1343", "73", "2262", ggplot2's default order solid, "22", "42", "44", "13", "1343"). Of the extraction work
read, LineEX matches legend entries to lines with a learned image embedding, LineFormer leaves the matching for later,
ChartOCR has none; WebPlotDigitizer's and Engauge's documentation say nothing of legends or line styles. No published
method was found that matches series to legend entries by a MEASURED dash pattern (searched, not surveyed in full).

Where PlotTracer fell short, measured on a corpus made for it (algorithms/__tests__/fixtures/findLines/dashes,
dashes.py: eleven figures, series sharing one ink in matplotlib, ggplot2 and R styles; converging, parallel and
crossing curves; one grey, one with two inks of two styles each; each series' pixel polyline and legend sample):
1. Find lines read solid and dashed series whole (94-100%), dash-dot mostly (68-99%), and dotted ones hardly at all
   (0-51%): a dot is too small to have a direction, so the dash-gap pairing never reaches it, and every dot was filed
   as label ink. On the bundled release figure the dotted curve came back in nine pieces.
2. Auto-extract matched a legend entry to its series by colour only, so on one ink it read every line into the first
   entry ("Formulation A 2" to "8") and skipped the rest.
3. Where the curves start together (the release figure's origin) the pieces are knotted; a few short pieces follow no
   series at all.

### What was built, layer by layer

1. Find lines reads dotted lines (algorithms/findLines/dots.ts): blobs no bigger than a dot, standing on paper, each
   linked to the next at the same spacing and in about the same direction, a row of six at least; rows broken where
   another line swallows a dot are joined end to end. The dots are measured points, the spaces between them bridged.
   A dot must stand on paper: a fleck of this colour against another ink is that ink's anti-aliased rim (it made a
   false row along the blue line of xy-multiseries-modulus). On the corpus every dotted series is now read along
   96-100% of its length in at most two pieces (algorithms/__tests__/findLinesDotted.test.ts).
2. A line's style is measured (algorithms/lineStyle.ts) the same way on a legend sample and on a found line: the ink
   along the path a pixel at a time, exact along it and one pixel of tolerance across it. A style is: solid or not;
   one kind of mark or two; the period (a mark and a gap, or a whole cycle); the share of the period that is ink. A
   line whose long unbroken runs make up most of its length is solid, whatever it picked up at a crossing.
3. The lines are given to the legend entries by style: the well-measured lines are grouped by style first, the groups
   paired with the entries by the least total difference (so a short, ambiguous legend sample is settled by the
   others), and short pieces go to the group whose style they share. On the corpus: no line that follows one series
   is given to another; 93% of the length of the lines that follow a series is given to it, the rest left unassigned
   (algorithms/__tests__/lineStyle.test.ts).

# v2.6 - design cases

Gate 1: a design is finished only when every case reads "given X, the screen
shows Y". Gate 2: an agreed case becomes a failing test, named for the case,
before any implementation. ⚠️ Gate 3: this page is NOT evidence that anything
does what it says - only a test of the same name is.

Status: **AGREED** (David) · **TEST** (a named test exists and was seen failing
first) · **OPEN** (a decision still owed).

## Why v2.6 is a Contour type and not curve separation

The first draft of these cases (A1-A11, curve separation) was rejected by
David, and rightly. Before anything was designed, every idea was measured
against today's tools (plottracer-ref, `research/v2.6-today/`). Guide points already capture
every same-ink crossing, dash, marker, label gap, occlusion, band edge and
contour line correctly. By colour and Flood-fill capture the different-colour
cases and contour lines. What was missing for contours was the RECORD: a level
could only be typed into a series name.

## Contour - agreed 2026-10-07

The record was checked against the libraries that draw contours (R
`contourLines()` is exactly `{level, x, y}` per line; ggplot2
`geom_path(group, colour = level)`; matplotlib `ContourSet(ax, levels, allsegs)`;
plotly only as line traces, its Contour trace needing a z grid).

- **C1 - AGREED · TEST.** A contour line, captured with any of today's tools, is
  one series carrying a numeric level. Its name stays free text.
  (`contourType.test.ts`: "C1 a contour line is a series that carries a numeric
  level…", "C1 the level survives saving and reopening the project")
- **C2 - AGREED · TEST (record) · UI to come.** Several lines can share one
  level, and the data sheet groups them under it.
- **C3 - AGREED · TEST.** The export is one row per point: line, level, x, y, in
  drawn order. Grouping by level gives matplotlib's `allsegs`. Guide-point roles
  ride along as a `Point` column; an interpolated level adds `Level from`.
  - **OPEN:** a closed contour must end on its first point (R and matplotlib
    both store it that way). Today's record cannot close a line. A repeated click
    on the start is slotted in beside it, and traced loop ends land 9-14 px apart
    while a traced dash's ends can be 3.6 px apart, so no distance rule decides it.
    Named as `it.todo` until David decides how a line becomes closed.
- **C4 - AGREED · TEST (blank + guard) · interpolation to come.** Unlabelled
  lines get interpolated levels:
  - The user drags a line across the contours to give their order.
  - Between two labelled lines, the k unlabelled lines in between get equal steps
    of (b - a) / (k + 1), for any k, so uneven labelled levels are fine.
  - Beyond the last label, the neighbouring stretch's step continues.
  - It is refused only at a turning point (where order cannot tell up from down),
    and the screen says why.
  - A line with no level shows "level not set" and exports blank, never 0.
  - An interpolated level looks like interpolated points do, and stays editable.
- **C5 - AGREED.** Levels for several selected lines can be filled as start +
  interval: offered, never assumed. Uneven levels are typed.
- **C6 - AGREED, then REPLACED by F4 (David's bridging rule, 2026-10-07).** A
  line cut by its own label is ONE line, the stretch under the label a fair
  interpolation. Pieces whose continuation is not clear stay separate lines.

**Open, David's to place:** the Contour card is appended on a 16th row of the
picker (Heatmap is its data relative).

## Find lines - AGREED 2026-10-10 (David), as a FIRST VERSION

David, 2026-10-10: *"we should ship a first version and that is what we are going
to call it as well"* - and because it will not be perfect, the user gets an
intuitive way to correct it (K1-K6 below). Edge cases are accepted for now; where
the first version is unsure it SPLITS and marks the place, so nothing it gets
wrong is silent.

Agreed for the first version (David, 2026-10-10):
- Find lines serves BOTH contours and same-colour XY curves (one pipeline).
- Labels are read with the reader the app already ships (tesseract.js). A read is
  OFFERED as "from label" and corrected in the Level field like any value.
- Where lines merge closer than the pixels separate (N1), the first version
  SPLITS there and shows the stretch as unresolved; the user joins if wanted.
- A hand-captured contour line is closed by the same ink check as a found one.
- The Contour card stays on its own picker row for now.

**Why.** On the bundled contour example, neither of today's automatic tools takes
one line at a time with the defaults (David, driving the package). But the line
colour, kept inside the plot box and split into connected pieces at tolerance 120,
gave 9 large pieces for 7 lines. Each piece was exactly one line, and never two
mixed. The two extra pieces are the open lines that their LABEL cuts in two. The
small pieces are the label digits. That is one measurement on one clean figure,
so the cases below must also hold on the reference figures (F12).

The gesture words are first-pass placeholders, to be changed by David's hands.

### What the screen shows

- **F1 - AGREED. One press finds every line.** Given the bundled contour example,
  calibrated, when the user picks the line colour and presses **Find lines**,
  every contour line becomes its own series: seven on this figure. Each line
  starts as "level not set" unless its label is read (F5).
- **F2 - AGREED. Never two lines in one.** No series holds points from two
  different contour lines.
- **F3 - AGREED. Never the frame.** No point is taken from the plot frame, its
  ticks, or anything outside the plot box. That includes a line that ends on the
  frame.
- **F4 - AGREED. A label is bridged.** Where a line is cut by its own label, the
  two pieces become ONE line. The stretch under the label is filled by a fair
  interpolation between the pieces, and those points look like interpolated
  points do. Pieces whose continuation is not clear are not joined; the screen
  marks the place.
- **F4b - AGREED. A label belongs to the line it cuts.** David: on same-colour
  contours, which is the common case, the inline label is the separator - the only
  thing on the page that says which level a line is. A label is tied to a line
  because it sits in the gap BETWEEN TWO ENDS OF THAT LINE. Where a label sits
  between the ends of two different lines (a crowded plot), it is not tied to
  either; the screen marks it and says why, rather than guessing.
- **F5 - AGREED · CORE (not optional). The label is read.** The label found in a line's gap is read as
  that line's level, with v2.4's OCR, turned to the line's direction there (the
  label-angle control). The Level field shows the value as **read from the
  label**. It stays editable, like every measured value.
- **F6 - AGREED. An unreadable label says so.** A label that cannot be read
  leaves the level "not set". The screen says which line and why. C4's
  interpolation (start + interval, or between labelled lines) can then fill it.
- **F7 - AGREED. Label text is not data, and it is not hidden.** The pieces that
  are label digits never become points of a line. They are shown as "label"
  where they sit, never silently dropped.
- **F8 - AGREED. A loop is recorded closed.** A line whose ink has no free end
  inside the plot box, including after a label is bridged, is recorded as
  closed. Closure is MEASURED from the ink, not decided by a distance. (This
  answers C3's open closure question for found lines. Lines captured by hand are
  still open.)
- **F9 - AGREED. The press has an ending.** After Find lines, the card states
  what happened: lines found, gaps bridged, labels read, labels not read, pieces
  not used. A line the user does not want can be removed like any series. One
  Undo removes the whole result.
- **F10 - AGREED. Discoverable.** On a Contour figure the Auto-extract card offers
  Find lines first, with a prompt saying what it does ("finds every line of this
  colour, each as its own line, and reads its label"). By colour and Flood-fill
  stay available.
- **F11 - AGREED. Coloured contours.** Given contours drawn in a colour per level,
  picking one colour finds that level's lines. Picking several colours, or
  repeating the press per colour, finds the rest.

### The level axis (contour is 2.5D: the level is a coordinate on a third axis)

- **L1 - AGREED. The level axis has a scale.** The Contour card offers **Level axis:
  Linear / Log**, as an XY axis offers Log X.
- **L2 - AGREED. Interpolation in the axis's scale.** C4's equal steps between two
  labelled lines are equal in that scale. On a log axis, one unlabelled line
  between 1 and 100 is 10, not 50.5.
- **L3 - AGREED. A colour key calibrates the level axis.** On a coloured contour
  plot with a key, the key is calibrated with the heatmap's colour-key steps, and a
  line's level is its position on the key. Recalibrating the key moves every level
  with it.
- **L4 - AGREED. Contradictions are refused at the axis.** Two labels read on one
  line that disagree, or a nested line whose level breaks the order of its
  neighbours, are flagged on screen. The levels are never silently changed.

### How the data is stored

Checked against what a plotting library needs to redraw the plot from the
saved data alone: R `contourLines()` is `{level, x, y}` per line, with a closed
line ending on its first point; matplotlib `ContourSet(ax, levels, allsegs)`
needs the same, grouped by level.

- **S1 - AGREED.** Each found line is one series. Its points are ordered along
  the line, and the bridged points are included, so the line is continuous.
- **S2 - AGREED.** A bridged point carries the interpolated role, so the export's
  `Point` column says `interpolated`. A reader can always tell what we measured
  from what we filled in.
- **S3 - AGREED.** The level is stored with where it came from: `label` (read),
  `typed`, or `interpolated`. Today's `Level from` column gains `label`.
- **S4 - AGREED.** A closed line is exported ending on its first point, exactly
  as R and matplotlib store it.
- **S5 - AGREED.** What the label said, and where it sat, is kept with the line
  as the evidence for its level. It is not exported as a data point.

- **S2+ - AGREED.** A point's role is measured, bridged or shared. A SHARED point
  lies on a stretch two lines run along together, and is recorded in both.

### Where the first version is unsure (from the 2026-10-09/10 research)

- **N1 - AGREED (first version: split).** Given lines that run together closer
  than the pixels separate, the merged stretch is shown as UNRESOLVED ink, the
  lines entering it end there, and the place is listed in Places to check.
- **N2 - AGREED.** Given a label read that is unsure, has lost its decimal point,
  has a different number of decimals from the figure's other labels, or that the
  lines around it contradict, the level stays "not set" and the screen shows what
  was read and why it was refused.
- **N3 - AGREED.** Given read levels that are not a whole number of steps apart,
  nothing is interpolated and the screen says the levels are uneven.
- **N4 - AGREED.** A region the user masks is an occluder: a line passing behind
  it may be bridged across it, drawn as bridged.
- **N5 - AGREED.** Given two same-colour curves that run together and part, the
  stretch is recorded in BOTH lines, its points marked shared. Where which side
  continues which cannot be decided, the pieces stay separate.

### Correcting it (David, 2026-10-10: "a mechanism or workflow for the user to correct things in an intuitive way")

- **K1 - AGREED. Places to check.** After Find lines, the right-hand panel lists
  every place the first version was unsure: bridges made, pieces left split,
  unresolved ink, labels not read or refused. Clicking one takes the view there.
  It stays on screen while the user corrects, and a fixed entry drops off.
- **K2 - AGREED. Cut.** A rail tool beside Erase: click a line where it should be
  cut, and it becomes two lines there. Greyed, with the reason, until there are
  points.
- **K3 - AGREED. Join.** In the series panel, "Join with" another line: the two
  become one, the gap between their nearest ends bridged by the usual fair
  interpolation and drawn as bridged.
- **K4 - AGREED. Move.** In the Data points section, "Move selected points to"
  another line, using the selection the Select tool already makes.
- **K5 - AGREED.** Two curves swapped at a crossing are fixed with Cut and Join -
  no separate tool.
- **K6 - AGREED.** One Undo removes a whole Find lines result, and one Undo
  reverses each Cut, Join or Move.
- **K7 - AGREED. It is called a first version.** The Find lines button carries a
  "first version" tag; the release notes and the manual say so.

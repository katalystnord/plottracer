## See the figure on its own

Everything PlotTracer draws on a figure - data points, calibration reticles,
category ticks, grid dividers, error caps, measurement lines - can now be taken
off in one press. There is an **eye button in the top bar, beside the zoom
control**; press it and the figure is shown exactly as the paper printed it, with
none of our ink on top. Press it again and everything comes back precisely where
it was.

It is there for the moment you want to check what is actually underneath a mark:
a tick label a divider is sitting over, a hatch you are not sure of, a faint
gridline you suspect is a data point.

**While the marks are off, the figure is inert.** Nothing can be placed, grabbed
or selected, and the tools that would do those things are greyed rather than
merely ignoring you - each one says why on hover, and names the way back.
**Panning still works**, because looking around the figure is the entire purpose.
Your data is untouched throughout: the table on the right goes on showing every
point you have captured.

Hiding the marks belongs to the figure you hid them on, so opening another figure
always shows its marks.

## The category axis is measured, not declared

A bar chart's category axis is now read off the bars themselves when nothing
declares it, instead of relying on a count you type. Measured on the synthetic
hatched corpus, this took **automatic** bar recall from **36.7% to 45.5%**. On
published figures it was unchanged, which is the honest other half: the gate was
chosen so it could not cost real bars.

## Span charts

A bar that floats clear of the axis - a temperature range, a tornado chart, a
diverging figure - was always a bar with an asterisk: it shared the bar model
because nothing else fit, and the model had to bend around it. It is a type of
its own now, **Span chart**, sitting beside Bar and Histogram on the graph-type
picker.

**Bar now means exactly what its name says: measured from a baseline.** Its
value is the far corner of the drag, read against the origin the figure's own
category axis sits on - not a number you type. A bar that does not reach that
axis still records both of its measured corners and reports its reading, with a
note explaining that it does not reach the baseline: either the click fell short,
or the figure is a Span chart. Nothing is ever silently dropped.

**A Span reports both measured ends, sorted smallest first, and no single
value** - `Min` and `Max`, because a span has no baseline to be signed against.
Capture is the same drag as a bar's: one corner to the opposite corner.

**A saved project whose bars all float opens as a Span chart, and says so.**
Bar's model changed under it, so a figure captured the old way is read as what
it now is - and because that is an inference from the pixels rather than a fact
the file states, the app tells you it made it.

**Stacked segments can be negative, and now say so.** A segment's own height is
never negative - the mark on the page cannot be - but which side of the origin
the figure drew it on can, and a stacked chart of profit and loss draws losses
downward from zero. That segment now reports its true, signed value instead of
reading as a gain of the same size, and the record carries its own base as well
as its contribution - `Base` and `Value` - so a stack traced one colour at a
time still keeps its place.

**The bar table can be edited in place.** Double-click a value and type a
correction: it moves the measured corner along the axis rather than overwriting
a number, the same rule PlotTracer's other editable tables already follow, and
the edited reading is marked as yours.

**A figure drawn across the plot - a zero rule cutting a diverging chart in
two - is put back together before it is read**, the same repair that already
puts a hatched bar back together. Traced by colour, a floating-temperature
figure with bars on both sides of zero came back as five extra fragments before
this; it now reads as the twelve bars the figure actually shows.

## Error bars on each end of a span

A span has two measured ends, and each of them can now carry its own error.
Drag from the low end out to its cap, then from the high end out to its own -
the same gesture as everywhere else in PlotTracer, done twice. Each end gets its
own columns in the table and in every export, named after the reading they
belong to: `Min SD upper`, `Max SD upper`.

**PlotTracer refused this until now, and the reason it gave was wrong.** It said
a span with uncertainty at each end is really a candlestick. It is not: a
candle's wicks are the highest and lowest prices actually traded, where a span's
caps are uncertainty about a measurement. Same shape on the page, different
claim, and recording one as the other would file four measurements as
two-plus-a-doubt.

**Which end a cap belongs to is measured, not asked.** You start the drag on one
of the two ends, so the tool already knows which one you mean. And because a
span reports its ends sorted, the error is sorted with them - the `Min` column
can never carry one end's value beside the other end's uncertainty.

## Candlestick charts

**Candlestick is a graph type of its own**, sitting beside Box Plot on the
picker. You mark four places per candle, walking up it - the low, the lower body
edge, the upper body edge, the high - prompted one at a time, and an overlay is
drawn over the figure showing the candle you just recorded, exactly as a box
plot's is.

**The prompts name places, not values,** because a slot name would point the hand
at the wrong place half the time: on a falling candle the "open" is the TOP body
edge. The position of each mark is the same whichever way the period moved, so
that is what you are asked for.

**The direction is measured from the figure, not from your click order.** A
candle's colour is what tells a reader whether the period rose or fell, and the
two body edges are geometrically identical - the same rectangle either way round
- so colour is the only signal the figure carries. Rather than assume a
convention, PlotTracer reads the bodies and finds the figure's own two
appearances: filled against hollow, red against green, whatever this figure
actually drew. Which of the two rises is one fact per figure, not one per candle.

**One tick corrects a figure drawn the other way round.** The measured default is
offered, never imposed - if this figure's rising candles are the other colour,
say so once and every candle follows. The setting is saved with the project.

**The overlay fills a falling candle and leaves a rising one hollow,** which is
what lets you check the reading: a candle drawn filled against a hollow one in
the figure underneath is the disagreement, and the tick is what answers it.

A candlestick refuses error bars, for the same reason a box plot does: its four
values already are the period's extent, so there is no single value for an error
bar to be measured from.

## Stacked bar charts

A stacked bar chart is its own type now, because its segments are connected. A
segment's bottom is never a number of its own: it **is** the top of the segment
below it, and the lowest segment sits on the baseline. The edge the figure draws
once is measured once, where before it was captured twice, as two series' corners
a few pixels apart.

That makes the reported `Value` a **height** rather than a position on the axis,
which is the difference from a bar. A segment running from 2 to 5 reads 3, with
its base of 2 shown beside it. Type a number into it and it means *this segment
is that tall*: the segments above move up and keep their own values, because
their bases moved with them. You cannot change one without the other, because
there is only one number there.

Which way a column stacks comes from the figure rather than a setting. The
segment nearest the baseline is the first, and each one after it is the next edge
outward, so a stack drawn downward from zero, or one with segments on both sides,
needs nothing switched on.

`Bar` loses its last mode with it. It had a **Stacked bars** checkbox that
quietly changed what the columns meant, what an edit did and what the record was;
a bar is now measured from a baseline and nothing on the card changes that
answer.

## The box plot joins its family's table

A box plot marks a category axis in the same walk a bar chart does, and then
listed its boxes in capture order in a panel of its own. It now files its five
values under those categories, in the same table Bar, Span and Candlestick use -
one row per category, one column per named value.

Nothing about the record changed. The panel had been keyed to the two-corner
drag that Bar and Span share rather than to the question it was actually asking,
which is whether a type files its readings under categories.

## The graph-type picker, reordered

The picker is grouped by what the data IS rather than by what the chart looks
like. A pie now sits beside Bar, because a pie and a bar carry the same record -
a category and one magnitude - and the same figure is often drawn either way.
A histogram moves away from Bar despite looking like one, because it reports a
distribution, which is a box plot's kind of claim.

The bar family now reads as a block: Bar and Span above Box Plot and Candlestick,
so one measured value against several named ones reads down, and simple against
compound reads across.

## Polar: a tilted or squashed figure

The polar calibration card asks one new question, **Shape - Circular, or Tilted
or squashed.** Circular is the default and the walk is unchanged.

Choose tilted when the figure's rings are ellipses rather than circles, which
happens when a chart has been rescaled to fit a column or photographed off a
page. The walk then also asks for P2's angle, at a different angle from P1, and
for the radial value at the centre; with those, readings are taken through the
shape the figure was actually drawn in. Read as a circle, such a figure is wrong
everywhere at once with nothing on screen to say so.

## Smaller corrections

**A ternary diagram is read from the triangle you clicked.** All three corners
are used now, so a diagram drawn as a right-angled triangle reads correctly
instead of being read as though it were equilateral. Equilateral figures, which
is nearly all of them, read exactly as before. Three corners in a line are
refused.

**A heatmap's grid stays on the ink it was measured from when a calibration value
is corrected on a logarithmic axis.** It already did on a linear one.

**A circular chart recorder refuses two radial clicks at the same distance from
its centre**, which left the radial scale with nothing to measure.

**A WebPlotDigitizer project this tool cannot calibrate is now refused by name,
rather than opening and reading zero everywhere.** That tool accepts some
calibrations PlotTracer does not - two axis ticks carrying the same value, or a
log axis through zero - and such a project used to open looking perfectly
ordinary and export a sheet of zeros.

**A number you typed into a heatmap cell now follows its own patch of figure when
the grid changes.** Add or move a boundary and your reading stays on the cell you
read it from; when a change splits or merges that cell, the reading is dropped
and the card says so rather than moving it to a cell nobody looked at.

**The armed-pipette banner moved to the bottom of the canvas**, where the
calibration card no longer covers it.

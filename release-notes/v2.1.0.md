# PlotTracer v2.1.0

Category ticks for bar charts, a corrected spider reading, and a large amount of
work on the parts of the app that write your numbers into a file.

## Category ticks — give the category axis a geometry

Calibrate the value axis as before. A **"Mark category ticks?"** fold-out then
appears on the folded calibration card — one arrow and four words, which is the
whole cost to anyone who does not want it. Open it, click once, type how many
categories the figure has, and ticks appear along the category axis: draggable,
with the two axis ends shown and frozen, because every tick is a function of them.

- **A bar's category is now its BAND**, derived rather than stored — so adjusting
  a tick re-homes the bars instead of leaving them where a guess put them.
- **A merged run of touching same-colour bars is cut** at the dividers you
  declared, and each piece is measured by the median of its own columns rather
  than by its extreme.
- The geometry round-trips through the project file and survives image edits,
  undo and reset.

⚑ **What this fixes, and what it does not.** It removes a real defect: a
fabricated category name when the first series you captured was the incomplete
one. Measured against 882 published bar figures, it does **not** improve
auto-extraction recall — it fixes 48 bars and breaks 44, and the reason is now
diagnosed: the cut is at the wrong granularity, because most merges happen
between series *inside* one category rather than between categories. The wider
retake is scheduled rather than guessed at.

## Spider auto-extract read every value high

Auto-extracting a radar chart returned every value out by one **marker radius** —
uniformly, on every axis at once, which is what made it look like a calibration
problem rather than a geometry one. Measured on the bundled example at **+5.18 px
across 18 readings**.

The reading is now decided from the ink rather than assumed: a run reaching the
hub is a filled shape and reads at its boundary; a short detached run is a line
or a marker and reads at its middle; a long detached run is a spike's wedge and
reads at its boundary again. Mean error is now under 1.5 px, with seventeen of
eighteen readings inside 0.7% of their axis.

**If you traced a spider chart with auto-extract before this release, reopen it
and re-extract — the values were high.**

## Exports

- **The JSON export now carries the Δ for an error series**, beside the absolute
  cap positions it already carried. The other formats had a delta column all
  along; JSON, the format written for machines, was the one that made a reader
  re-derive which cap belongs to which point.
- **Excel exports no longer fail** when two series share their first 31
  characters — "Absorbance at 450 nm, replicate 1" and "… replicate 2" produced
  the same sheet name twice, and the whole workbook was lost.
- A geometry block no longer arrives with a blank series name under Active scope.
- Turning geometry on for a series no longer deletes that series' curve fit and
  error-bar relation.

## The Trace Challenge

The bundled examples' hidden game gains **spider and pie** rounds, and now draws
a graded four-round game — two easy, one medium, one hard — across twelve rounds
and seven scoring families.

## Under the hood

`Workspace.tsx` is 26% smaller (9,142 → 6,782 lines) with eight new engine
modules at 99–100% mutation coverage, and a 14-defect audit was run and closed
before release. Six modules in the export and import layer — the code that turns
your work into a file — were re-tested against mutation: five rose from 65–74%
to 85–100%, and the sixth from 92% to 96%. That is what found the Excel failure
above, the missing JSON delta, and two dead guards that could never fire.

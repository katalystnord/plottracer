A bar chart now says where its categories are before it lets you capture one, the
error-bar model was rebuilt around the datum that owns it, one click / two clicks
became the same gesture on every output panel, and the release was audited until
it stopped finding things.

## A bar chart is calibrated in one walk, and the categories are part of it

Marking the category axis used to be a fold-out you could find afterwards, if you
knew it was there. **It is now the second half of the calibration walk**: two
clicks for the value axis, two for the outer edges of the first and last
category, and the number of categories typed on the last one. Four steps, one
card, and the card is the heatmap's card - the same two stages, the same folded
one-line summary when it is done - because a second thing that behaves the same
way should look the same way.

**Capture refuses until the walk is finished**, and says which step is waiting.
Before, a bar chart would happily take readings with no category axis behind
them, and each reading then guessed its category from where it landed relative to
its neighbours. A bar's category is now the band its own pixel falls in.

The first end of the category axis is **offered** at the value origin you already
clicked, because on most figures that is the corner where both axes meet. It is
an offer: drag it and it is yours.

**A project that arrives half-calibrated resumes where it stopped.** A bar chart
imported from WebPlotDigitizer has a value axis and no category axis, and the
walk simply picks up at the first category step rather than declaring itself
finished.

Gone with the rebuild: the entry button, `Re-place axis`, `Remove ticks`, the
separate count box, the canvas marking mode that quietly took over clicks in
every tool, and the red warning that was pinned somewhere other than the control
it warned about. **A tick mark and the handle you grab it by are now one object**,
here and on the heatmap, so the thing you can see is the thing you can move and
neither can drift away from the other.

## What a bar reports, and when it reports two numbers

**A bar whose near end does not sit on the baseline reports both of its measured
ends** - `Min` and `Max`, in two columns under the series - instead of a single
`Value` that would silently mean the low end on one row and the high end on the
next. The question is measured off the pixels, not assumed from the fact that a
baseline was declared: a figure can have both a declared baseline and floating
bars, and `samples/bar-floating-temperature.png` is one.

The capture slots say **`Min` and `Max`** in the file too. `Bar start` and `Bar
end` implied an ordering the record deliberately does not keep - which corner you
dragged from is not a property of the bar.

**`Stack group` is gone.** It was a free-text field whose value nothing ever read,
described by a tooltip for behaviour the code did not have, and leaving it blank
on a stacked figure silently measured every segment from zero. Stacking is now
one checkbox on the calibration card, asked once about the figure.

**A bar drawn across zero is one bar.** Where a figure paints its zero rule over
the bars, the ink is severed and each half used to arrive as its own reading -
twelve months came back as seventeen. Both halves are rejoined across the
declared baseline, and the trace line says how many it joined.

**One reading, one number.** A bar's value is its far end restated against the
baseline you declared, so `Value` and `Max` can no longer sit in the same row
disagreeing in the fourth decimal. A bar's measured width reaches the file at the
figure's own resolution rather than as sixteen digits claimed from a pixel.

## When two readings land in one category

Trace a bar chart and two shapes can land in the same category band - a legend
swatch beside the bar it collides with, or a second colour traced into one
series. The first one captured used to take the cell and **the other had no row
at all**, just a sentence saying a reading was not shown.

**Both candidates now get a row**, tinted together so they read as a pair, with
the neighbouring categories drawn back as context so you can see which value fits.
Either can be selected on the figure or deleted, and the actions name the series
they belong to rather than acting on whichever happens to be active.

**And where every category is doubled, the answer is not deletion.** That is a
second colour traced into one series, where every reading is real - so the panel
says so and offers the remedy instead. Better still, the offer now arrives at the
gesture: pick a colour that differs from the one behind this series' readings and
Auto-extract offers to **trace into a new series**.

**A legend swatch is named rather than filed silently.** A swatch is a filled
rectangle in exactly the series ink, so it matches at any tolerance. Shapes that
do not reach the baseline and are much smaller than the bars that do are reported
as suspects - every box is still captured, because a technique that deletes a
measurement without saying so is the worse half of the same problem.

## Error bars belong to their datum

An error bar used to be a separate series related to its parent by name, and
which cap belonged to which point was worked out at read time, every time. That
resolution was invisible when it was right and invisible when it was wrong.

**A cap is now a member of its datum's own row.** The four roles - upper, lower,
left, right - are appended to whatever slots the graph type already owns, so an
XY point carries `Value` plus four and a bar carries `Bar start`, `Bar end` plus
four. The tuple says which cap belongs to which reading, so the drawing and the
record cannot disagree: neither of them is inferring anything any more.

**The handle end IS the cap.** There is no separate ball marker to drift away
from the whisker it belongs to, because there are no longer two objects that have
to agree. Caps are always draggable - you no longer have to make a series active
before you can move one.

**The app stops naming the error for you.** The kind of error - SD, SEM, 95% CI -
is printed in the figure's caption, not drawn in its geometry, so we could only
ask you to type it, and asking means offering a default. That name becomes the
export column heading, so an invented one would ride into your file.

**Error bars work on every graph type that has points**, and on the types with a
value axis we can measure - XY, Bar, Box Plot, Histogram, categorical Line - the
cap is pinned to the line its datum's value runs along, so it cannot lean. On the
types whose geometry cannot say, the cap is simply free.

## One click selects, two edit - on every panel

Every output panel now answers a click the same way: **one click selects the row
and rings it on the figure, a double click opens the editor for the fields that
type offers.** Bar, Box Plot, Pie and the histogram's bins could not do the first
half of that at all before - a table of twenty bars could not tell you which bar
on the figure it meant.

The editor now occupies **the value's own box**, so opening one moves nothing
else on screen. Names are click-to-edit rather than permanent input boxes: a
name you are not required to give should not sit there as a field demanding one.

## Which numbers came off the pixels

A value you typed rather than measured now reads in `[square brackets]` on
screen and carries a `<field> source` column into the file. You are not the only
instrument looking at the figure - a hatched cell, an asterisk over a fill, a
label bleeding into the ink - so a reading you take by eye is a reading, recorded
through the same transform as ours. What the record distinguishes is measuring
from inventing, not you from us.

**Where you can type one, in this release: XY charts, spider profiles and
heatmap cells.** On Bar, Box Plot, Histogram and a categorical Line you can name
a category but not type a value, so the bracket mark and its `source` column
cannot appear there. That is a capability those types have not been given yet
rather than a mark that has gone missing, and it is the piece the v2.4 label
reading needs before it can offer you a printed number to accept.

## A categorical Line measures its category

A Line on a categorical axis stored a value and worked out its category from
left-to-right capture order. Capture a figure whose second category has no point
and every later point silently moved up one. It now sits on a banded axis and its
category is the band its own pixel falls in - a function of that pixel and the
calibration and of nothing else, which is what lets a plotting library place it.

## The Trace Challenge

The bundled examples ship ground truth, and the Trace Challenge scores a trace
against it. It is a way to find out how good you are at this, and a way for us to
find out where the tool makes it hard.

## An eye-dropper, alongside colour as a measurement

Colour became something this release measures, and a plain eye-dropper came with
it. Pick it up in the Measure tool, click a pixel, and it records the colour it
read - and where a colour key is calibrated, the value that colour stands for.
It is a convenience, not a new instrument to learn: a reading you could otherwise
only take by eye, written into the same measurements block as distance, angle,
area and slope.

## Smaller, and the re-audit

The calibration prompt no longer vanishes at the moment it is needed. A reused
corner stops telling you to click it again. The heatmap card stops saying one
thing three times. A file action that fails says so instead of looking like it
worked; cancelling an export no longer marks your work saved.

A bar's error whisker starts at the bar's **centre**, where figures draw it,
rather than at the corner you happened to drag from. Once a calibration stage is
committed its labels come off the figure and only the handles remain, so the
picture you are measuring is not covered by the marks that measured it. A
half-finished bar stops swallowing the next drag, and a category tick stops
swallowing a drag that begins on the baseline. A cap is reported to the same
precision as the reading it qualifies, and the data panel rounds at the figure's
own resolution, as the file already did.

Then the release was audited twice - once by hand, once by a fleet - and forty
findings were fixed before the tag. The ones worth naming, because they were
wrong in the FILE rather than on the screen:

- a stacked or grouped **bar chart** valued every series but one by the wrong
  rule, and which one was right depended on what was selected when you pressed
  Export;
- one error cap on a **spider** chart dropped every reading but the first spoke
  from all nine export formats, while the panel went on showing them all;
- **error columns** reached the tables and not the JSON on bar, box plot and pie,
  and reached nothing at all on the histogram;
- a **figure switch** carried the previous figure's heatmap grid and hand-taken
  readings into the next figure's record;
- the **colour key** was sampled once and reused against the next figure's image.

A project file's tuple positions are now bounded at the load door, a typo in a
colour box is refused instead of quietly meaning black, and a series name from
someone else's file can no longer reach a spreadsheet as a formula.

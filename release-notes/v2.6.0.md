## Find lines - every line of one colour, each its own line

Many figures draw several curves, or a whole contour plot, in **one ink**. Until
now that meant tracing each line by hand: every automatic tool took all the
pixels of that colour and merged them into one series.

**Auto-extract ▸ Find lines** (first in the card on XY and Contour figures) does
it in one press. Pick the lines' colour - the pipette takes the line's own ink,
not the pale edge under the cursor - and press **Find lines**. Every line of that
colour becomes its own line, in a colour of its own on screen, even where
same-colour lines **cross**, **touch**, **run together** or are **cut by their
own labels**.

- A gap the figure drew no ink in - a label, a dash, a crossing - is filled by a
  fair interpolation between the line's ends, and those points are marked
  **bridged** in the export's `Point` column. A stretch two curves share is
  recorded in **both**, marked **shared**. Nothing filled in passes as measured.
- **Where it is unsure, it does not guess.** It leaves the pieces apart and lists
  the place under **Places to check**, below the data on the right: lines that
  met and were joined, pieces left apart, a line that stops inside the plot, ink
  too merged to separate, a label it could not use. Click one and the view goes
  there.
- A legend inside the plot: mask it first with **Image ▸ Mask an area**. Every
  reader leaves it out, and Find lines also carries a line across it.
- **Find lines again** replaces what the last press found - but keeps any line
  you renamed or gave a level.

It is called a **first version**, and the button says so. It will sometimes get a
line wrong, which is why the next section exists.

## Putting a line right

- **Cut** (rail, beside the eraser): click a line where it should become two. A
  closed line opens at the cut instead.
- **Join with** (series panel): joins the line you are on to another at their
  nearest ends; the gap is bridged and marked. Two lines with different levels
  are refused.
- **Select** now works across lines on XY and Contour: a box or a lasso takes
  the points of **every** line inside it, ringed on each, and Del removes exactly
  those. **Click any line** to make it the active one - no hunting in the list.
- **Move N selected to** another line, slotted in where each point sits.
- The **Eraser** takes a box too: drag it over stray bits and they go from every
  line at once.
- A line captured with **Guide points** is left alone by all of these - its
  guide points are its record, and it is edited through them as before.

One **Undo** reverses any of these, or a whole Find lines result.

## Contour plots - a new chart type

**Contour** joins the graph-type picker. A contour line is a line that carries a
**level**: a number beside its points, not text in its name, so a library can
redraw the plot from the export alone (matplotlib's `ContourSet`, R's
`contourLines()`). Several lines can share a level; a line with no level exports
a blank, never 0.

- **The labels are read.** On a labelled contour plot, Find lines reads each
  line's own label and **offers** it beside the line's Level - *"The label reads
  0.4  [Use]"*. It is offered rather than written in, because the reader gets
  some labels wrong, and a wrong level looks exactly like a right one. A read it
  refused says why.
- **Fill in the other levels** gives unset lines their level in equal steps from
  the ones you set - and says so instead, when the levels are not equal steps.
- **A colour key calibrates the levels.** Tick **Colour key** in the Contour
  calibration and click the key's two corners and two labelled ticks: each line
  then takes its level from where its own colour sits on the key, shown *from
  colour key*. Recalibrating the key re-reads those levels. One picked colour
  finds that level's lines and no other's.
- **Log level**, beside Log X and Log Y: the level axis has a scale, and filled-in
  levels follow it.
- **A line clicked all the way round closes itself** - when the gap back to the
  first click is the line's own ink, never just because the ends are close - and
  ends on its first point, the way R and matplotlib store a closed contour.
- The data table groups lines **under their level**.
- The export says where every level came from: `Level from` is `typed`, `label`,
  `key` or `interpolated`.

## Fixed

- The stress-strain example draws the whole curve its truth file claims.
- A confidence band is not covered by the error model yet, and the code now says
  so rather than implying otherwise. Bands get a model of their own in a later
  release.

## Under the hood

Find lines runs in the background, so the window stays responsive on a large
scan. Everything runs on your machine; no figure leaves it.

PlotTracer 2.0.1 — error bars, and three numbers that were quietly wrong

A patch release, and worth reading rather than skipping: three of these were
producing wrong values on screen and in exported files, on figures the app
otherwise handled perfectly.

Every one was found the same way — by using the app on real figures and
checking the result against the bundled ground truth, rather than by any test
or analysis tool. The suite, the mutation run and the type-aware lint passes
were all green throughout.

On a bar chart with no declared baseline — a temperature range, a tornado
chart, anything whose bars float — a bar's value is its **span**. It was being
signed by the order you happened to click the two corners: top-left downward
gave you −12 where bottom-left upward gave +12, for the identical bar.

Two people digitizing the same figure got different signs, and neither number
was more correct than the other. Worse, the negative came from the *natural*
gesture: dragging down from the top-left is how most people draw a box.

A span is a magnitude. A bar from −10 to −5 spans 5, not −5 — its *position* is
negative, its *extent* is not. Floating bars now report the span, whichever
corner you start from. Bars measured against a declared baseline are unchanged:
there, the sign is real and comes from the figure.

Placing an error cap locks it to the bar's axis, so the whisker stays true to
its data point. Adjusting the cap afterwards — which the tool explicitly tells
you to do, to move a mirrored cap where the figure actually draws it — did not.
A drag with any sideways component pulled the cap off its datum, giving a
leaning whisker and a recorded X the figure never showed.

The lock now applies wherever a cap moves: drag, arrow-key nudge, or typed
value. A cap slides along its own bar and nowhere else.

The data panel showed every cap as its own X and Y — three columns of which the
X was, by construction, always a copy of the data point's X. Nobody reads a
figure that way, and no plotting library takes its input that way either.

An error series now shows a single **Δ** column: the cap's signed offset from
its data point. Reading across a row gives exactly what you would type back
into matplotlib or Excel to redraw the figure — `x`, `y`, `−Δ`, `+Δ` — and an
asymmetric bar's two arms are tellable apart at a glance.

Exports carry **both**: the absolute cap positions, which are what was measured
off the pixels and remain the record, and the deltas beside them. matplotlib
and Excel want the deltas; ggplot's `ymin`/`ymax` want the absolutes. Neither
reader should have to do arithmetic on someone else's record.

Nothing about the saved file changed. The deltas are computed when read, from
two measured ends — subtraction, not an assumption.

The tick across the end of an error bar was 16 pixels wide, sitting on a data
marker 14 pixels across. Two near-identical widths, one on top of the other, so
the end of the whisker dissolved into the point. It is now clearly wider than
the marker it has to be read against.

Every bundled error-bar example was symmetric, which hides the one real trap in
the workflow: the app seeds the second cap as a mirror through the data point,
so on a symmetric figure it happens to land correctly and you never learn that
an untouched cap is a starting position rather than a measurement.

**Error bars — asymmetric 95% CI** is lopsided at every point, because
time-to-failure data genuinely is. Open it beside the ± SD example and the
difference is the whole lesson.

The manual gained a full **Error bars** section — it previously said only "rail
tool 6". It covers the two fields, why the drag snaps at the data-point end and
never at the cap end, upper/lower versus left/right, the mirror, how to move a
cap where the figure draws it, several kinds of bar on one point, what deleting
does, and what export writes.

Every screenshot on plottracer.com was recaptured on 2.0. Some still showed a
control that v2.0 replaced. Two claims on the site described a disclosure the
app does not actually make, and have been corrected rather than kept.

Installers for Linux (.AppImage, .deb), macOS (Apple Silicon .dmg/.zip) and
Windows (.exe) are attached below. The builds are **unsigned**, so macOS and
Windows will warn on first launch; open from the context menu on macOS, or
choose *More info → Run anyway* on Windows.

PlotTracer works fully offline. Your figures never leave your machine.

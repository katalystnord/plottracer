PlotTracer 2.0.0-rc1 - bar charts become a first-class record

Every digitizer treats a bar chart as a scatter plot you happen to click on the
top of each bar. That is not what a bar is. A bar is an **interval** - it runs
from somewhere to somewhere, and both ends carry meaning. Read as a single
point, a floating bar loses its lower end, a stacked segment reports the total
instead of its own contribution, and a bar below the baseline comes back
positive.

PlotTracer 2.0 records the interval.

**A bar is captured by dragging corner to corner.** Click one corner, a live
rectangle follows the cursor, a second click fixes the opposite corner. The
same gesture captures a plain bar, a floating one, a grouped sub-bar and a
stacked segment - there is no mode to find and nothing to switch.

- **Bars that share a baseline.** Tick *Bars share a baseline* and type its
  value. Each bar is then measured from that baseline to its far end, in value
  space rather than pixel space - which is what makes a bar **below** the
  baseline read negative rather than being flipped by hand.
- **Floating bars.** Untick the baseline and there is nothing to sign against,
  so the drag direction carries the meaning: corner-to-corner up is positive,
  down is negative. A tornado chart or a monthly temperature range works
  directly.
- **Stacked bars.** Each segment is its own drag-box, and naming a *Stack
  group* on the series tells the app they belong together. A stacked segment
  reads as its own span, never as a running total - the one number a
  point-based reading cannot give you.
- **Horizontal bars.** One checkbox. The category axis follows onto Y and
  everything else is unchanged.
- **Log value axes**, and a value axis that does not start at zero.

**Categories are shared, not retyped.** The names live in one canonical list
that every series reads from, so renaming a category in one place renames it
everywhere - and a new series' bars are prefilled from whichever named bar
they actually sit under along the category axis, not from the order you
happened to click. Where the pairing is ambiguous the cell is left **blank**:
a name you fill in yourself is honest, a wrong name that looks transcribed is
not.

The data panel shows one row per category and one column per series, so a
grouped chart reads the way it looks on the page. The old panel showed one
series at a time and hid the rest.

**Auto-extract by colour now works on bars, histograms and box plots.** Sample
a bar's colour and the detector proposes bounding boxes for every bar that
matches; the proposal is yours to accept, correct or discard. It is an assist,
not an oracle, and *How well does it work* below says exactly where it stops.

**Box Plot and Histogram joined the same vocabulary.** A histogram bin is a
drag-box whose two corners are the bin's top edge, so its height is one
measurement rather than an extent; a box plot's five slots stayed as they
were, but the shared tuple language, the shared table and the shared category
list now cover all three.

**Pie charts** gained the same category treatment as bars - a slice's name is
click-to-edit, shows a dash at rest, and is never invented.

**Choosing a graph type is now a card grid** with an icon and a description
for each, rather than a dropdown whose entries you had to already know. The
Open Example list is two columns with figure icons, and the tool hints that
used to be stuffed into example names moved into the hint bar where hints
belong.

## How well does it work

v2.0 was measured against the **ICPR 2022 CHART-Infographics** benchmark -
open-access PMC papers with human-verified ground truth, not synthetic charts
and not figures chosen to flatter the app.

- **Manual drag-box capture recovered the exact value for 31 of 32** figures
  traced by hand. That includes a genuine log-scale axis and two genuinely
  stacked charts, one vertical and one horizontal.
- The one that did not match is not a defect: it is a chart where the bar's
  *length* encodes a percentage while a *printed number* beside each bar
  encodes a raw count, and the benchmark's ground truth uses the printed
  number. The bar's length is read correctly. Reading the text would need
  OCR, which PlotTracer does not have and does not fake.
- **Auto-extract by colour was measured over all 192 bar figures in the split
  - 3,234 real bars**, not a sample, with no region restriction. The result
  depends almost entirely on two properties of the figure itself, and both are
  worth stating plainly:

  | the figure | recall |
  |---|---|
  | has colour, bars don't touch | **98.1%** |
  | has colour, bars touch | 81.6% |
  | greyscale, bars don't touch | 69.0% |
  | greyscale, bars touch | 63.9% |
  | **all 192 figures** | **76.9%** |

  Colour detection needs a colour, so a greyscale or hatched figure is outside
  what it can do - axis lines, hatching and error-bar caps share the bars' own
  tones. Across figures that do carry colour, recall is **83.9%**.

  The other limit is ours and is worth naming: **bars of the same colour that
  physically touch flood into one region and are read as one oversized bar.**
  That is what separates 98.1% from 81.6%, and it is the single change that
  would most improve auto-extract.

  Where it does not reach, manual drag-box capture does - exact on 31 of 32
  figures traced by hand - and the two sit side by side rather than one
  pretending to be the other.

Three new worked examples ship with the app - grouped, stacked and floating
bars - each with its committed ground truth.

## Fixed

- **A tilted or 3D pie ignored the total and the sweep you gave it.** Every
  slice was read against 100 and a full circle instead - so a tilted pie of a
  different total was scaled throughout, and a tilted half pie read every value
  at half. The slices still summed to the total, so nothing looked wrong.
- **A bar chart's declared baseline was not saved.** Reopening a project put
  every bar back on a baseline of zero, so a floating bar recorded as 5 came
  back as 7.5 - the two ends were right and only the value changed.
- **A multi-series bar chart exported only the series you were looking at**, to
  every format, while the table on screen showed them all. The Active/All
  choice is now offered for bar, box plot and pie as well.
- **Deleting a bar left its category behind** as a row you could not fill or
  remove, and retyping that name produced two rows with the same name.
- **An XY or histogram axis accepted the same value at both ends** and then read
  that one number everywhere.
- **Shift-clicking a data point replaced the selection instead of adding to it.**
- **The magnifier now draws at your display's real resolution** rather than
  being smoothed up to it.
- **Several ways to lose unsaved work with no prompt**: cancelling the Save
  dialog, switching figures, and dropping or pasting an image all left the app
  believing the document was saved.
- **A half-finished straighten survived leaving the image editor**, after which
  every click was recorded a few pixels from where you clicked.
- **A lasso drag that left the canvas** disabled zooming and swallowed the next
  click.
- **The fitted curve absorbed clicks meant for the points underneath it.**
- **A project folder whose name contained brackets** could make Open Project do
  nothing at all.
- **Values that cannot be a number** (infinities, undefined results) no longer
  break the MATLAB, Python and LaTeX files they are written into, and a label
  can no longer act as a formula when a CSV is opened in a spreadsheet.
- **A map scale of zero calibrated cleanly and measured everything as zero.**
  Draw a scale bar, type `0` for its length, and every distance and area read
  exactly 0 with no error shown. Now refused, in words that name the
  requirement and its consequence.
- **A log axis calibrated through zero reported success and then read
  nothing.** The click path had refused this for some time; opening a project
  file or importing one from another tool did not. Both doors now refuse.
- **Five axes classes indexed calibration points without checking how many
  there were**, so a hand-edited or truncated project file reached them.
- **Bar, Polar and Circular Chart Recorder calibrations reported success on
  input that made every reading null or a single constant.**
- **A pie's exploded-slice state was missing from the undo snapshot** - undo
  or *Reset calibration* mid-explode left a stale slice index that silently
  swallowed every subsequent click, with nothing on screen to say so.
- **Deleting a series left the point selection pointing at it**, so the arrow
  keys nudged a point in a different series.
- **Deskewing by 180° or 270° lost the outer ring of pixels** and, at 180°,
  could return a 5×5 image as 4×4 padded with blanks.
- **The by-colour trace region survived undo/redo across an image edit**, so a
  region drawn on the old picture was applied to the new one.
- **A power-law fit's refusal claimed a requirement it does not enforce.**
- **Curve geometry produced nonsense on fewer than two points.**
- **The spreadsheet and exports fabricated a "Series N" name** where the real
  one was missing, rather than leaving it blank.
- **An ODS table name could exceed the format's 100-character cap** after a
  deduplication suffix was appended.
- **Auto-extract listed its points in blob-scan order**, not reading order.
- Several contrast and layout defects, including About text clamped to less
  than half the width of its own card.
- `npm start` disabled the Chromium sandbox on every platform when only Linux
  needed it.

## Under the hood

The test suite is now 2067 unit tests plus a 294-test Electron end-to-end
suite, and mutation testing runs across `core/`, `algorithms/` and `engine/`
rather than `core/` alone. That instrument is what found most of the
calibration defects above: several were code paths no test reached at all, and
one was a refusal the code already contained but could never execute.

Dead code inherited from the original project was removed rather than left to
look load-bearing - an entire unused selection API, a write-only set of
calibration label fields, and an export builder nothing called.

---

**This is a release candidate.** The bar model is complete and measured against
real published figures, and this build goes out for use before 2.0.0 final.

It arrives after an unusually hard look at itself. Mutation testing was run
across the whole of `core/`, `algorithms/` and `engine/`, and then seven
independent reviews were run over the code - one per surface, each briefed with
this project's own history of recurring mistakes. Between them they found
defects that the test suite, the mutation score and the end-to-end suite had all
passed over, including two in features that had already shipped. Every one of
them is fixed above, each with a test that fails without the fix.
